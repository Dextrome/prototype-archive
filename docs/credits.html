FROM THE DEVELOPMENT NOTEBOOK

Media notes
& credits.

All imagery is selected from game development renders or clearly labeled staged concept scenes. Film editing, compression, crops and typography were prepared for this archive. No claim is made that a concept film demonstrates a finished game.

What the film labels mean

Gameplay trailer: captured from a running build, sometimes with bots or scripted ordinary player inputs. Forward cuts may compress time.

Staged concept film: authored sequences communicate a proposed experience. The five factory films use deliberately scripted interactions, combat, outcomes and progression. Hellgrinder stages its machine and danger meter.

Gurk’s extended feature trailer includes an original synthesized chamber score and edited sound effects from its CC0 game bank. The 7 Ants Underground trailer includes an original synthesized electronic score and native procedural game sound effects. Updated showcases for ShpinkyDinky, Squabblobs, Murderbox, Mazacre TD and Meldkin retain their native game sound effects. All remaining films are silent. Screenshots and films may come from different development passes. Current game descriptions are grounded in project feature documents and source; an older capture can show an earlier interface or art treatment.

Gurk trailer production notes

The extended feature trailer uses the real Gurk renderer and existing core mechanics. Starting conditions, body and trap placement, the demonstration save, cameras and timing are staged for readable shots. Cleaning, rinsing, hauling, recycling, disposal, traps, pickups, gadgets and purchases use the game’s rules. It is a montage, not a continuous player session. The trash-radar interface is enlarged for legibility. Capture runs offscreen and does not record the desktop.

Audio: original synthesized chamber score for this trailer. Edited game foley is derived from CC0 recordings by rubberduck (OpenGameArt), Wareya (RPG Foley), and Kenney (50 RPG sound effects), as documented in Gurk’s audio provenance.

ShpinkyDinky trailer production notes

Four authored setups replace the previous random bot match. They demonstrate an attached-bomb kill, a bounce-pad redirect that hits, a goo-slowed escape caught by a bomb, and a vent pushing the opponent into a prepared bomb trap. An isolated capture copy supplies scripted player inputs, staged starting positions and close cameras. The existing device simulation, movement, redirects, damage and deaths resolve the outcomes. Raw device/network identifiers are replaced by readable tool and fuse labels in the capture copy. Timing is slowed for legibility, and native game sound effects are retained. The original game project is unchanged.

Goblin Ballistics timed flippers

The new gameplay trailer replaces the simultaneous timer-driven flippers with independent, ball-aware inputs. Each flipper is tapped briefly only when the approaching ball is within its near reach, and both rest during upper-playfield action. The edit contains four left taps and two right taps, with no simultaneous presses. Two excerpts from one run retain native physics, attacks, scoring and game timing. The input director runs in an isolated project copy; this is automated gameplay, not a claim of human play. The trailer is silent.

Sobriety Kills combinations & overdose

The new 46-second trailer covers six pairings across the seven-substance slice catalog. Four combat setups demonstrate mixed attack animations, enemy slowdown and the native merge action. Two fatal pairings begin with an empty overdose meter and reach cardiac arrest or respiratory depression through the existing simulation, followed by the existing death animation and result screen. An isolated capture copy stages wave-four stashes and adjusts framing, figure scale, HUD text contrast and meter colors. Activation, merging, attacks, damage, overdose accumulation and results are native. The edit uses closer combat crops and an inset of the native HUD. The editorial overdose readout follows the captured game state; the overdose buildup and collapse play at half speed. The film is silent. These risk labels and effects describe the fictional game balance.

Squabblobs arsenal & four-player gameplay

The 44-second edit opens with a 27-second montage retaining all nine demonstrated weapons and powers: Charge Bow, Lob Bomb, Guided Missile, Enlarge Ray, Shrink Ray, Meteor Slam, Boulder Form, Ricochet Beam and Gravity Well. Native drafts play at 2× or 3.2× speed; gameplay retains normal speed with forward cuts, including a cut ahead to the gravity-well collapse. The final 16 seconds show three four-player free-for-alls on Snowy Arena, Volcano and Classic Arena. All four slots use the game’s unchanged BotBrain, ordinary player-input handling and normal ability rules; no attacks, hits, eliminations, terrain damage or round results are injected. Loadouts are selected for the recording, spawn points are native, and camera framing is tightened. Earlier weapon demonstrations retain staged starts and scripted inputs. The offline hit-stop clock follows fixed recording frames so its intended brief duration is preserved. The original project is unchanged. Native procedural sound effects follow the edit with level adjustment.

Murderbox modules & synergies showcase

Six controlled encounters demonstrate all three weapons and 14 of the 16 module types. An isolated copy stages starting inventories, legal tile placements and enemy spawn queues using the native Run and CombatSim classes. Weapon damage, adjacency bonuses, energy, heat, repairs, shields, Demon Reactor damage, disables, vent blasts and kills resolve through unchanged game rules. Native source stats drive the explanations. The Splitter section includes the actual in-game hover tooltip; the other module-tip panels, outlines and chapter text are editorial overlays. Forward cuts retain normal game speed. Native procedural sound effects are retained with cuts and level adjustment. The original project and its pre-existing local edits are preserved.

Mazacre TD run progression

A chronological edit of nine completed waves on the native Pocket seed, 474084x18y18@v6. The existing maze and partition planners choose legal structures; their decisions are replayed one at a time through native build, selection and command-card handlers. A separate native simulation checks that each replay reaches the planned wallet, structures, local levels and research state. Piledriver specialization, two additional local damage levels, three speed levels and first purchases in all four research categories are curated decisions, paid for with the run’s earned minerals. No currency, combat outcomes, waves, health, kills or results are injected. Combat uses the game’s visible 4× speed; forward cuts skip unshown play and several planning frames are held for readability. Editorial colors mark checkpoint groups and an analyzed choke with four route crossings. The bot uses a shorter, 40-iteration design search; all original Core, UI and bot code is unchanged. The original project is preserved. Native audio includes the game’s licensed Sonniss GDC 2024 and Kenney CC0 sound effects, cut and level-adjusted for the film.

Ant Delve dig, mine & return showcase

The 52-second edit follows one orange worker through a complete gameplay loop: a straight tunnel tile connects the route, the worker mines one sugar on each of three turns, walks home and banks three points. A soldier then enters an antlion chamber; native combat defeats the antlion and leaves the soldier at 50/150 HP. The soldier also mines one fungus through the same end-turn rules.

An isolated capture copy stages the starting board, veins, rosters and stationary guard, with ambient predator spawning disabled. Every subsequent placement, movement, turn, resource, score and combat result comes from validated commands in the unchanged native simulation. The rival colony passes and its idle turns are omitted. Camera zooms and holds are authored; the combat log is hidden. Combat sprites are separated horizontally and ants draw above the pit for legibility. Combat beat spacing increases from 0.34 to 0.80 seconds, with longer damage-number holds; movement timing is unchanged. Captions are editorial, and the cargo/score readout follows recorded game state. The original game project is preserved. The film is silent.

Meldkin builds & progression

The 67-second trailer shows three native tower combinations across Interchange and Geode, all four buyable organisms, the tower inspection panel and Needleback’s full upgrade sheet. Crater and Ravine are bought through the existing in-run purchase handler. The separate permanent Upgrades screen buys Seed gold I, unlocks Mortarcap and buys Quicken, with the XP balance falling from 30,000 to 16,500 and purchases saved to an isolated profile. Later waves, legal placements, starting funds, lives and XP are staged; attacks, projectiles, layer stripping, culling, kills and purchase rules use unchanged game code. Forward cuts keep normal gameplay speed. Chapter text is editorial, the panels are native, and procedural game audio is retained with cuts and level adjustment. The original project and player saves are preserved; only the capture copy’s map textures are regenerated after adding its director.

7 Ants Underground rope & toybox showcase

The 36.2-second trailer uses the current OreilChaos renderer and native simulation in an isolated project copy. Controlled inputs demonstrate ninja-rope firing, retraction, release and reattachment, a real airborne Burrow Rocket kill, Tunnel Bore digging, a three-shot terrain breach, a Spring Mine launch, a Mandible Clamp wall-splat, Belly Bounce knockback, the native 2.5-second Echo Toybox projectile replay, rising Panic Toybox lava, and a four-ant Toybox encounter. Starting positions, visual classes, loadouts and the breach room’s additional cover and footing are staged. The Panic excerpt begins nine seconds into its existing lava schedule. Movement, rope forces, projectile collisions, terrain edits, damage, deaths and hazards resolve through the game’s existing rules; no hits or kills are injected. Forward cuts remove downtime; gameplay plays at normal speed. Cinematic framing and hidden HUD are capture choices, and captions are editorial. Native hit-stop uses the fixed movie-frame clock to preserve its intended short duration during offline rendering. The source game and its existing local edits are unchanged.

Audio: an original synthesized electronic score created for this trailer, mixed with the game’s native procedural sound effects. No external music or audio assets are used.

Rot Garden Romance garden-to-date gameplay

The 60-second gameplay trailer follows one Blood Turnip from planting through a real harvest choice into Mira’s Cemetery Picnic. Reckless Compliment joins the native starter deck and is played during the encounter. Five card plays and one end turn produce 27/25 affection, a native win, Mira’s Bond progression and a new seed reward before returning to the garden. No affection, win, reward or relationship result is injected.

An isolated capture copy and save profile use scripted UI inputs and a selected deterministic shuffle. Mira has already been met and the tutorial is complete at the start. The existing debug growth advance skips eight minutes, disclosed on screen. Original dialogue and choices remain intact; text reveal and line advances are grouped before longer reading holds. Crop and deck shots are cropped closer, the debug growth button is hidden, and native card focus styling identifies the next play. Chapter captions sit outside the game viewport. The original game and player saves are preserved. The film is silent.
