FROM THE DEVELOPMENT NOTEBOOK

Media notes
& credits.

All imagery is selected from game development renders or clearly labeled staged concept scenes. Film editing, compression, crops and typography were prepared for this archive. No claim is made that a concept film demonstrates a finished game.

What the film labels mean

Gameplay trailer: captured from a running build, sometimes with bots or scripted ordinary player inputs. Forward cuts may compress time.

Staged concept film: authored sequences communicate a proposed experience. The three factory films use deliberately scripted interactions, combat, outcomes and progression. Hellgrinder stages its machine and danger meter.

Gurk’s extended feature trailer includes an original synthesized chamber score and edited sound effects from its CC0 game bank. The 7 Ants Underground trailer includes an original synthesized electronic score and native procedural game sound effects. The ShpinkyDinky tactics trailer includes an original synthesized score and native game sound effects. Updated showcases for Squabblobs, Murderbox, Mazacre TD and Meldkin retain their native game sound effects. Hellgrinder’s full-run trailer retains its native procedural audio. Collision Course retains its synthesized game audio. All remaining films are silent. Screenshots and films may come from different development passes. Current game descriptions are grounded in project feature documents and source; an older capture can show an earlier interface or art treatment.

Gurk trailer production notes

The extended feature trailer uses the real Gurk renderer and existing core mechanics. Starting conditions, body and trap placement, the demonstration save, cameras and timing are staged for readable shots. Cleaning, rinsing, hauling, recycling, disposal, traps, pickups, gadgets and purchases use the game’s rules. It is a montage, not a continuous player session. The trash-radar interface is enlarged for legibility. Capture runs offscreen and does not record the desktop.

Audio: original synthesized chamber score for this trailer. Edited game foley is derived from CC0 recordings by rubberduck (OpenGameArt), Wareya (RPG Foley), and Kenney (50 RPG sound effects), as documented in Gurk’s audio provenance.

ShpinkyDinky trailer production notes

The supplied 27-second ShpinkyDinky-Tactics-Trailer.mp4 is used unchanged. Three authored encounters demonstrate five banked hits around cover, an attached bomb following an opponent behind a wall, and a vent reversing a pursuer into a bomb blast. A control run uses the same movement without the vent: the opponent escapes at full health. The comparison makes the vent’s effect visible.

An isolated capture copy uses scripted player inputs, staged starting positions and simplified collision geometry. Native movement, device placement, projectiles, physics, fuse timing, damage and deaths resolve the outcomes. Health pips, accumulated bolt paths, the bomb radius and captions explain actual game state. Brief freezes explain placements; the vent reversal uses 2.5× slow motion. The original game project is unchanged. Audio combines an original synthesized score with native game sound effects. The archive’s screenshots show the earlier capture.

Goblin Ballistics timed flippers

The new gameplay trailer replaces the simultaneous timer-driven flippers with independent, ball-aware inputs. Each flipper is tapped briefly only when the approaching ball is within its near reach, and both rest during upper-playfield action. The edit contains four left taps and two right taps, with no simultaneous presses. Two excerpts from one run retain native physics, attacks, scoring and game timing. The input director runs in an isolated project copy; this is automated gameplay, not a claim of human play. The trailer is silent.

Squabblobs arsenal & four-player gameplay

The 44-second edit opens with a 27-second montage retaining all nine demonstrated weapons and powers: Charge Bow, Lob Bomb, Guided Missile, Enlarge Ray, Shrink Ray, Meteor Slam, Boulder Form, Ricochet Beam and Gravity Well. Native drafts play at 2× or 3.2× speed; gameplay retains normal speed with forward cuts, including a cut ahead to the gravity-well collapse. The final 16 seconds show three four-player free-for-alls on Snowy Arena, Volcano and Classic Arena. All four slots use the game’s unchanged BotBrain, ordinary player-input handling and normal ability rules; no attacks, hits, eliminations, terrain damage or round results are injected. Loadouts are selected for the recording, spawn points are native, and camera framing is tightened. Earlier weapon demonstrations retain staged starts and scripted inputs. The offline hit-stop clock follows fixed recording frames so its intended brief duration is preserved. The original project is unchanged. Native procedural sound effects follow the edit with level adjustment.

Mazacre TD run progression

A chronological edit of nine completed waves on the native Pocket seed, 474084x18y18@v6. The existing maze and partition planners choose legal structures; their decisions are replayed one at a time through native build, selection and command-card handlers. A separate native simulation checks that each replay reaches the planned wallet, structures, local levels and research state. Piledriver specialization, two additional local damage levels, three speed levels and first purchases in all four research categories are curated decisions, paid for with the run’s earned minerals. No currency, combat outcomes, waves, health, kills or results are injected. Combat uses the game’s visible 4× speed; forward cuts skip unshown play and several planning frames are held for readability. Editorial colors mark checkpoint groups and an analyzed choke with four route crossings. The bot uses a shorter, 40-iteration design search; all original Core, UI and bot code is unchanged. The original project is preserved. Native audio includes the game’s licensed Sonniss GDC 2024 and Kenney CC0 sound effects, cut and level-adjusted for the film.

Ant Delve colony turns & four-player gameplay

The 86-second trailer opens with eight consecutive complete turns across four rounds of the same two-colony match, played at 1.5× speed. Each colony places four tiles. Their workers mine three sugar and three seeds, then walk home and bank three and six points. A replacement worker keeps mining while a soldier clears the antlion chamber. Short captions mark placement, movement and end-turn results. The old recap and end card are removed.

The final 24 seconds show a separate four-player native AI match at 6× speed. The standard Slice5 map, seed 75, normal camp draft, default rosters, predator rules and unchanged GreedyAi planner supply the gameplay. The camp draft completes before the excerpt starts. A fixed camera fits the entire map between the native HUD and ant cards; it does not jump between colonies. The excerpt advances into round nine with 34 new tiles, 34 completed turns and banked scores of 2/2/2/5. Every replayed AI turn is checked against the planner’s projected simulation checksum. The speed and new-match transition are disclosed on screen, and the finale’s round, tile and score readouts follow its own audit.

An isolated capture copy stages only the opening board, veins, rosters and stationary guard, with ambient predator spawning disabled for that opening. Subsequent placements, movement, turns, resources, scores and combat resolve through validated native commands. No turns are omitted from the opening; it is retimed continuously. The original capture view adjustments remain: the combat log is hidden, mixed-cell sprites are separated horizontally, ants draw above pits, combat beats are spaced at 0.80 seconds instead of 0.34, and damage numbers are held for 1.20 seconds instead of 0.85 before the labeled edit speed is applied. Movement playback timing is unchanged in the captures. Captions are editorial and native state drives the counters. The original game project is preserved. The film is silent.

Meldkin builds & progression

Forward cuts keep normal gameplay speed. Chapter text is editorial, the panels are native, and procedural game audio is retained with cuts and level adjustment. The original project and player saves are preserved; only the capture copy’s map textures are regenerated after adding its director.

The 36.2-second trailer uses the current OreilChaos renderer and native simulation in an isolated project copy. Controlled inputs demonstrate ninja-rope firing, retraction, release and reattachment, a real airborne Burrow Rocket kill, Tunnel Bore digging, a three-shot terrain breach, a Spring Mine launch, a Mandible Clamp wall-splat, Belly Bounce knockback, the native 2.5-second Echo Toybox projectile replay, rising Panic Toybox lava, and a four-ant Toybox encounter. Starting positions, visual classes, loadouts and the breach room’s additional cover and footing are staged. The Panic excerpt begins nine seconds into its existing lava schedule. Movement, rope forces, projectile collisions, terrain edits, damage, deaths and hazards resolve through the game’s existing rules; no hits or kills are injected. Forward cuts remove downtime; gameplay plays at normal speed. Cinematic framing and hidden HUD are capture choices, and captions are editorial. Hellgrinder full-run gameplay

The 94-second edit follows one standard five-shift run from the main menu through equipment procurement, contracts, directives, operation, invoices and the native success screen. It starts with the normal 160 Bloodcoin budget: standard cores, Receipt Printer and Hellfire Vent cost 120, leaving 40. Four contracts pass; the Clean Processing Order fails because purity is 65 against its 70 requirement, despite meeting the shipment target. The run adds a Pressure Valve, Greed Flywheel and Audit Scrubber, makes debt repayments, handles mid-shift choices and ends with 225 Bloodcoin and 350 debt. No inventory, funds, meters, timers, contract results or ending are forced. Automated inputs use the real UI buttons; the existing Balanced strategy is supplemented with curated late purges for disposal-contract inspections. Forward cuts keep normal gameplay speed. All chapter text and click rings are editorial; panels and outcomes are native. The rendered run matches its fixed-frame UI rehearsal exactly. The original project, including ongoing local edits, is preserved. Native procedural audio is cut and level-adjusted, and the full 1080p game frame remains visible above a separate caption strip.
